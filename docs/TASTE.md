# House taste (defaults)

Starting points for the art-director, not rules. A brief that says otherwise wins. These are the owner's preferences.

## Look
- Surreal, weirdcore, Y2K chrome, soft-tech, underground rave. Dimensional mandalas, recursive patterns, reflective
  materials, strong contrast, depth made with selective blur.
- **Every design gets its own palette identity.** Don't default to magenta, cyan and purple. Avoid generic neon and
  polished-AI-image sheen. Don't repeat one composition in different colors; if two concepts only differ by palette,
  they are one concept.
- For trippy work prefer a compelling pattern or central symbol over a literal fantasy landscape.
- No skulls where the owner has ruled them out (assume ruled out unless a brief says otherwise).
- "Edgy" is not automatically aggressive. Stylish, playful, flirty and a little trashy often fits better. Match the
  audience: a professional brand has to feel credible and reliable; rave artwork can be much stranger.

## Process
- Explore several genuinely different directions before polishing one (a concept sheet). If an idea isn't hitting, say
  why, not just generate more of the same.
- Once something is close, change it with targeted edits. "Improve this" never means abandon what was selected.
- Be honest when something looks generic, doesn't fit or needs work. Say what is missing and offer a better direction.
- Spend render time carefully: think, explore cheaply (`--quick`, `--mode check`), then refine the strongest option.

## Deliverables that are actually useful
- Correct dimensions and aspect ratios, real transparency when asked, readable typography, clean edges with room for cutting.
- Separate files when the owner will assemble the final design themselves.
- Say clearly which files are concept mockups and which are production-ready.
- Reuse strong artwork across formats and products (`--size` variants of the same piece).

## Words
- Copy and captions sound like someone from the audience, not a marketing template. Culturally believable for EDM and
  rave culture; humor can be unhinged or raunchy but respectful; no forced slang or generic hype.
